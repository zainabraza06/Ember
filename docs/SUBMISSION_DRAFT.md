## Section 1. Official Space Apps website submission

**Project name:** Ember

**Challenge:** Flame in Freefall: SI-Powered Fire Safety Insights from Microgravity Combustion Data

**High-level summary** (identical text is used in Section 2):

> Ember is a web-based explorer for NASA's microgravity combustion research. Its home screen maps every test NASA has run by oxygen and pressure, shows where nothing has been tested, and marks the planned lunar-base atmosphere, the spacesuit and Earth sea level. It lists and compares the 24 combustion investigations in NASA's Physical Sciences Informatics (PSI) repository, ranks them for a lunar base, rover, spacesuit or ISS-like cabin with a visible formula, summarizes effects computed from 769 harmonised tests with evidence-strength labels, and answers questions only from claims extracted from NASA reports and checked against the report text, refusing when nothing matches. A printable one-page brief cites every sentence. Built for students, researchers and engineers, every figure is traceable to a NASA record or a checked claim, and anything undocumented is shown as such. SI tools helped build the pipeline and extract the claims; each of the 255 public claims was then opened and checked against its source page by the reviewer (ZRM).

**Detailed description**

Ember answers the challenge's problem that NASA's combustion results are "hard to find, harder to compare, harder yet to interpret". It is aimed at students, researchers and engineers who need to see what was tested, under which conditions, and where the evidence stops.

*What it does* (all of it is in the deployed page)
- **Atmosphere map (home).** Every test with both oxygen and pressure (643 of 769) is a mark by oxygen share and total pressure; colour and shape show how it ended, with outcome classes that include a "stopped while still burning" class that is not counted as an extinction. Hatched areas have no test. The official lunar-base zone (oxygen 30% or more, 10.2 psi or less), the spacesuit condition, Earth sea level and the highest-oxygen CFI-G test are marked. A gravity switch shows 0 g, 1 g and, from one NASA report, three oxygen-limit bars each at lunar and Martian gravity (marked outside PSI). A panel on the lunar zone lists the tests inside (48, all CFI-G in microgravity), nearby tests, untested pressure and oxygen bands, and sources.
- **Investigations and comparison.** All 24 PSI combustion investigations with objectives, approach, conditions varied and measurements quoted from the PSI record, fuels, shape, size and oxygen, pressure and flow ranges from the unified test table where rows exist (6 investigations), and "not documented in the catalog" otherwise. Tags (prevention, detection, suppression) with reasons from the official text or record metadata, hardware groups, filters, and a side-by-side comparison of two or three investigations with same / differs / not documented highlighting.
- **Ranking.** A deterministic formula (docs/RANKING.md) scores the 24 investigations for a lunar base, pressurized rover, spacesuit or ISS-like cabin (the last envelope is the team's own definition). The breakdown into atmosphere match, safety area, fuel, data volume and gravity regime is visible for every score. It measures relevance, not scientific quality.
- **Interpretation.** Three effect summaries computed from the test table: FLEX droplets (oxygen and diluent versus extinction), ACME CFI-G gas flames (oxygen versus how tests ended) and Saffire-II (0 g versus 1 g for the same sample), each with counts, caveats, the recorded source conflict and an evidence-strength label from a fixed rule.
- **Ask.** Retrieval over the evidence ledger only: 255 public claims extracted from 11 NASA reports (181 passed the automatic checks; 74 were approved after an AI-assisted page check). Each claim's quoted text must exist in the report and its numbers must match, and a second model must agree that the quote supports the claim; each of the 255 public claims was then opened and checked against its source page by the reviewer (ZRM). The 7 rejected and 3 held-back claims are never shown, never described as checked, and never used to answer. If nothing relevant exists, the page shows a refusal card instead of an answer. Quotations are shown only for US Government works.
- **Mission brief.** A printable one-page brief for the lunar base in which every sentence carries a source number.
- **Moon safety margin (home, under the stat strip).** A small bar chart of the change in oxygen margin of safety from NTRS 20130010991, Table I (Mylar, Ultem and Nomex at lunar and Martian gravity, percentage points of O2 versus the 1 g limit; a negative value means the flame propagates at a lower oxygen concentration than in 1 g, as the paper defines it). Every number is read from the transcribed Table I rows; the card is tagged outside PSI and states that it is one paper, three fuels, different test criteria from the 1 g test, a warning sign and not a design value.
- **Where should NASA test next? (home, after the map).** A data-gap ranking of untested oxygen-pressure bands of the map's microgravity grid, limited to untested cells inside NASA's own planned lunar-base atmosphere from the challenge text (>= 30% O2, <= 10.2 psi). Adjacent untested cells in a pressure column are merged into bands (at most 15 points of O2 tall) and ranked by distance to the design point (30% O2, 10.2 psi), then by fewest tests in the cells around the band, then by oxygen (docs/RANKING.md section 7). The top five non-touching bands are cards; selecting one highlights the whole band on the map. It is a data-gap ranking, not a safety judgement.
- **Oxygen dial (home, after the Moon safety margin).** A keyboard- and touch-operable slider (15% to 40% oxygen, 44 px handle, markers for Earth air 21% and the lunar-base target 30%) over cards for the 84 transcribed 1 g flammability-limit rows (ULOI and MOC as printed; NTRS 20110008636 Tables 2 to 9 and NTRS 20170004702 Table 2) that state a numeric limit and a pressure (58 at 14.7 psia, 26 at 10.2 psia). Each card shows the material, its printed limits, the pressure, the source page and a state: below its limit (did not sustain flame in the standard test), above it (burned in the standard test) or, between the two printed limits, some samples burned. Cards that change state animate (not with reduced motion). Caption: "Limits from the standard 1 g upward-burn test, mostly at 14.7 psia. The planned lunar base is 30% oxygen at 10.2 psi. Pressure and gravity change the result; this is a warning, not a design value."
- **What a spacecraft fire does (home).** Four public claims from NASA's Saffire reports (NTRS 20240002981 and 20210011521) in a timeline, each shown exactly as stored with its review label and page citation, including the smoke-alarm threshold reached at 981 s with most hazardous gas already released. The ledger has no public claim on exposure limits or on temperature rising only near the fire, so those are not shown.
- **Judge mode.** The "25 s tour" walks through the lunar-base zone, an Ask answer with its citation, a refusal and the honest limits in 25 seconds with captions.
- **Also:** partial-gravity bounds (0 g and 1 g side by side for nine Saffire-II samples; the lunar and Mars gap), Earth applications (three NASA spinoff links) and an About page with the scope note and the ten official resources.
- **Design.** Dark default with a light option, keyboard navigation, text contrast of at least 4.5:1, a 390 px mobile layout, and loading, empty and error states.

*How the data was prepared.* A Python pipeline reads the PSI tables and hand-transcribed report tables into one table of 885 records (769 individual tests plus limit and series entries) with original values kept, a source reference on every row and quality flags. Automated checks fail the build on out-of-range values, duplicate ids or changed counts. A seeded 20-row spot check against the source files found no mismatches, and all rows pass an independent checker. Corrections where a report clearly contradicts a PSI table are flagged (`corrected_from_report`) and one genuine conflict is listed (`docs/CONFLICTS.md`).

*What it does not do.* The page itself runs no language model, so it does not write new answers, summaries or briefs: every sentence is quoted from NASA records, counted by code, or taken from a ledger claim. It does not rank scientific quality, does not model flammability (no predictive model was trained), and the effect summaries are associations in uncontrolled test series, not causal estimates.

**NASA data used**
- PSI Data Repository, Combustion Science (https://psi.nasa.gov/physci/repo/search?q=combustion): investigation records for all 24 combustion investigations (PSI-10, 20, 21, 22, 23, 25, 26, 39, 47, 60, 62, 68, 69, 98, 99, 100, 101, 102, 106, 107, 115, 117, 142, 159). Experiment tables from PSI-25 (BASS-II), 69 (FLEX), 98, 99, 100 (Saffire-I, -II, -III) and 159 (ACME CFI-G) were harmonised into the test table. PSI records are marked CC0-1.0 except one whose license field is empty.
- NASA Technical Reports Server reports used for transcribed values, cross-checks or limits: 20150023456 (FLEX), 20210011385 (BASS-II), 20170008805 (Saffire), 20010059376, 20130010991, 20250010653 (LUCI), 20110008636 and 20170004702.
- NASA Technical Reports Server reports used by the evidence ledger (claim extraction): 20150023456 (pp. 1-25), 20210011385 (pp. 1-109), 20170008805, 20210011521, 20240002981, 20010059376, 20130010991, 20250010653, 20110008636, 20170004702 and 20260002050 (surface vehicle atmospheric considerations).
- The ten resources on the challenge Resources tab are linked on the About page. The Researcher's Guide, the ScienceCasts video, the Apollo and Mir pages and the spinoff stories were linked, not read for data.

**Other data**
- One university thesis (M. Kim, 2022, University of Maryland) was read to confirm the meaning of two ACME CFI-G outcome labels; it is cited, not redistributed. No other third-party data.

**Use of artificial intelligence (SI)**
- **Team of one with AI assistance.** One person made this submission, with an AI coding assistant (Claude, via Claude Code) and the language models named below. 255 public claims, each opened and checked against its source page by the reviewer (ZRM).
- *SI-powered build.* An AI coding assistant (Claude, via Claude Code) wrote the pipeline, checks, tests, the ranking and interpretation code, the page and the documentation under the team's direction, and read report pages to transcribe tables. All of it is in the repository with its history.
- *Evidence ledger.* Google Gemini (free tier; models gemini-2.5-flash, gemini-3-flash-preview, gemini-3.1-flash-lite, gemini-3.6-flash) extracted claims from 11 NASA reports; a second model from a different family, OpenAI's open-weight gpt-oss-120b served by Groq, checked whether each quoted passage supports its claim. Independently of both models, code checks that the quote exists in the report text and that every number in the claim appears in the quote. Result: 265 claims were extracted from 11 reports; 181 passed every code check and the second-model check (shown on the page as auto-verified), 84 did not (mostly because the quoted passage does not itself state the gravity condition, or a number or condition could not be matched) and were held back for review, and none failed the quote check (the quoted text exists in the report in all 265 cases). On 2026-11-14 the held-back claims were decided in review by **ZRM (Zainab Raza)**, who considered the AI triage and page-check files as suggestions and checked the page herself: 49 approved as extracted, 25 approved with a corrected statement (the quoted span is unchanged and every corrected statement was re-checked by code against it; two of them, C-f4d3c81aef and C-cbca12ebcd, were approved by manual override of the number check: the first because the span's OCR is garbled, the second because the material and thickness come from Table 4, not the span), 7 rejected, and 3 still needing review. **255 public claims, each opened and checked against its source page by the reviewer (ZRM).** 181 passed the automatic checks first (code checks for the exact text span, numbers and conditions, plus a second AI model); 74 were held back by those checks and approved after an AI-assisted page check, which the reviewer then checked herself; 2 of the 74 were approved by manual override of the number check (C-f4d3c81aef, C-cbca12ebcd); 7 were rejected and 3 are held back, and none of those 10 is shown as checked. `data/review/claim_review.csv` records every review decision, the original wording and the AI suggestion that was considered. The 30-claim random audit in `docs/CLAIM_AUDIT.md` is not yet filled in.
- *BASS-II outcome labels.* The assistant read each of the 126 BASS-II test observations and proposed an outcome class with a confidence. These were **not produced through an API call**; they are the assistant's own judgements written into a script. **None has been approved by a human**, so none is used: every BASS-II outcome in the data is "unknown" until a person approves it. A planned independent second rating was not run.
- *At runtime.* The deployed page calls no model and makes no network request; all numbers are computed beforehand by code.

**Limitations**
- Only 6 of 24 investigations have per-test rows in the unified table; for the rest the page shows PSI record text only. 126 of 769 tests lack an oxygen or pressure value and cannot be placed on the map.
- Outcome classes: 369 self-extinguished, 38 burned to completion, 91 externally terminated (a censored outcome, not an extinction), 57 no ignition, 64 other and 266 unknown, which includes all of BASS-II. For FLEX the report's end-of-test label is used, and 58 of 280 tests have a label that disagrees with the recorded extinction diameter (listed for review).
- Ledger: the 84 held-back claims were decided in review by ZRM (49 approved, 25 approved with correction, 7 rejected, 3 still needs_review); the Gemini free tier (20 requests per day per model) forced the run to switch between models and limited it to the priority reports listed above (11 of the 22 reports in the source list); 3 claims still need review; claims in two reports (FLEX, BASS-II) cover only their narrative pages, not their per-test tables.
- The ISS-like cabin envelope in the ranking is the team's assumption. Tags and hardware groups follow the official text and record metadata; 11 investigations are untagged.
- Partial-gravity evidence consists of rows from two reports, is not PSI data, and cannot be drawn as flame tests on the map. The spacesuit condition has no test.
- Transcribed report values were spot-checked, not independently verified in full: all 10 transcribed values were AI-checked against the source PDFs, and 2 were also checked by ZRM; separately, the reviewer (ZRM) opened and checked all 255 public claims against their source pages. NTRS reports carry NASA rights determinations that are not open licenses, so they are cited, not redistributed.
